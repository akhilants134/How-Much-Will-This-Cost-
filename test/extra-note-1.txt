Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While some climate change is natural, since the mid-20th century, human activities have become the primary driver, mainly through the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and natural gas. These activities release carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases into the atmosphere, which trap heat and cause the planet to warm — a phenomenon known as the greenhouse effect.

The consequences of climate change are wide-ranging and increasingly severe. Rising global temperatures are causing polar ice caps and glaciers to melt, contributing to sea level rise that threatens coastal communities and low-lying island nations. More frequent and intense weather events — including hurricanes, droughts, wildfires, and floods — are already being observed worldwide.

Ecosystems and biodiversity are also severely impacted. Species that cannot adapt quickly enough to shifting habitats face extinction, and coral reefs are bleaching and dying due to ocean warming and acidification.

Addressing climate change requires both mitigation and adaptation strategies. Mitigation involves reducing greenhouse gas emissions by transitioning to renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, and hydroelectric power. Adaptation involves adjusting to the impacts already unavoidable, such as building flood defenses and developing drought-resistant crops.

International agreements like the Paris Agreement aim to limit global temperature rise to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels by encouraging nations to commit to emission reduction targets. Individual choices, government policy, and corporate accountability all play essential roles in determining the planet's future climate trajectory.
